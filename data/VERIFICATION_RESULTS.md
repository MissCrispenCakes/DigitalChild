## Summary

**Key Finding:** The conference presentation content (slides and detailed analysis PDFs) was based on thorough manual legal research. However, the **scorecard Excel files were not systematically synchronized** with that research.

**The Real Problem:**
1. **Manual research was superior** - The presentation materials show detailed, accurate country-level analysis (e.g., Botswana Act 2024 correctly identified in SADC slide)
2. **Scorecard files lagged behind** - Excel files used as data management tools weren't updated to reflect all manual research findings
3. **Geographic blind spots** - Both scorecard files have complementary but incomplete coverage, suggesting:
   - Different research sessions updated different countries
   - No systematic cross-file synchronization workflow
   - Manual research findings not consistently back-populated to scorecard files

**Critical Issue:** This is a **data management workflow problem**, not a research quality problem. The knowledge existed (evidenced by presentation content) but scorecard file maintenance didn't keep pace with manual research.

- **Manual research >> file maintenance** at that time
- **Key evidence:** Presentation slides correctly identify Botswana Act 2024, proving knowledge existed

### The Real Problem: Data Management Workflow
- **No systematic synchronization** between manual research and scorecard files
- **Multiple files maintained independently** without cross-checking
- **Geographic blind spots** from different research sessions updating different files:
  - MASTER: Albania/Algeria updated, Botswana ignored
  - Presentation: Botswana updated, Albania/Algeria ignored
- **No back-population protocol:** Research findings not systematically entered into data files

- **Pre-conference data freeze timeline** with systematic verification
